About

Bridging Grid Physics & Market Economics

Decisions about where large loads and new generation can connect are made by physical constraints first and prices second. Our research starts with both.

Manassas Energy Labs is an independent research initiative. We combine power-flow analysis and wholesale market data to examine how new technologies, economics, and transmission limits are changing how power systems are planned and operated.

Physics First

We start from power flow, thermal ratings, voltage limits, and contingency criteria (N-1, N-1-1)—the constraints that decide where large loads can actually connect.

Transparent Numbers

Every figure we publish carries a source and a date. When we model, we state our assumptions so readers can check them and substitute their own.

Decade-Scale View

We focus on the structural shifts of the next ten years: gigawatt-scale compute siting, advanced nuclear, transmission expansion, and market redesign.

        Active Project

        Battery State-of-Charge Forecasting

        Predicting whether a battery can deliver what it offered, before the grid operator calls on it.

        In progress

        Grid operators increasingly rely on batteries for evening peaks and reserves, but a battery can only deliver the energy it has stored. Owners prepare their market offers hours or a day ahead, and by the time the operator dispatches the battery, its actual state of charge (SOC) often differs from what the offer assumed. We are developing forecasts of battery SOC, and of the power and energy a battery can really deliver, so owners and operators can see shortfalls before they happen.

        Related focus areas: Prices & Market Design · Long-Duration Storage

        The Problem

        SOC drifts between offer and dispatch. Real-time dispatch departs from day-ahead schedules, regulation deployments add or drain energy, conversion losses and auxiliary loads such as cooling consume charge, temperature derates output, and degradation shrinks usable capacity. The market only sees telemetered SOC and the limits owners submit.

        Why It Matters

        When a battery can't deliver, the operator must replace the energy or reserves at short notice, often from more expensive units, and the owner can lose payments. CAISO's market monitor reports battery reserve awards becoming undeliverable in real time for lack of charge, and ERCOT has tightened how it accounts for SOC.

        Our Approach

        We model how SOC evolves from offer to dispatch, using operating data, market awards and dispatch instructions, reserve deployments, and weather. The output is a forecast of each battery's SOC and deliverable MW and MWh through the operating day, with uncertainty bands, flagging intervals where an award is at risk.

        Assumptions & sources
        • Grid capacity request = IT nameplate × design-day PUE. MVA assumes a 0.95 power factor. Design-day PUE values are our engineering assumptions; the global average PUE is 1.54 (Uptime Institute, 2025).
        • Annual energy = IT nameplate × average utilization × annual-average PUE × 8,760 h. Home equivalence uses 10,380 kWh per U.S. residential customer per year (EIA, 2024) and compares energy, not peak demand.
        • Capacity exposure = demand at system peak (IT nameplate × utilization × design-day PUE) × clearing price × 365. PJM: $325/MW-day, 2028/29 auction (PJM). MISO: $126.19/MW-day, 2026/27 North/Central annualized (MISO). Actual charges depend on peak-load contribution, reserve requirements, zonal scaling, and retail contract terms.
        • Flexibility: curtailment is a share of maximum annual consumption (grid capacity request × 8,760 h), the same definition used by Duke's Rethinking Load Growth (2025).
        • Peak timing: PJM sets capacity obligations from load in the five summer coincident peak hours (5CP) (BGE). ERCOT allocates transmission costs by 4CP (ERCOT); Texas regulators must revise that method by the end of 2026 under SB 6. "Avoidable" assumes curtailment covers every peak hour, which requires forecasting them.
        • Voltage classes are typical ranges only. The actual point of interconnection depends on the local network and the utility's planning criteria.
        • This is a screening tool, not an interconnection or facilities study.
        Analytical Framework

        Our Research Methodology

        How we turn power-system engineering into decisions that commercial and policy teams can act on.

        01

        Physical Grid Analysis

        We analyze AC power flow, N-1 and N-1-1 contingencies, thermal ratings, and voltage limits using public planning data and industry-standard simulation tools.

        02

        Market Overlay

        We overlay those physical constraints on wholesale market outcomes: locational marginal prices (LMPs), congestion and basis risk, and capacity auction results.

        03

        Decision Support

        We turn findings into executive memos, due-diligence reviews, and siting screens, and we publish the assumptions and sources behind every number.
